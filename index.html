I started out as an app developer. At the Apple Developer Academy I learned to build through Challenge Based Learning — starting from someone's actual problem rather than from a feature list — and shipped several apps to the App Store with teams of designers, planners, and engineers.

From there I moved toward XR. I built visionOS and ARKit projects, put objects into space, and made them respond to hands. But the more spatial apps I made, the more one gap stood out: everything I built could be seen and heard, and almost nothing could be felt. A virtual object you can pick up but not feel is still a picture. That gap is what pulled me into HCI — if I wanted interfaces that genuinely connect with people, I had to understand how people perceive, not just how devices render.

So I came to the Haptics and VR Lab at Kyung Hee University to study it properly. My research is on on-body haptic interfaces: instrumenting sensors and calibrating them into real physical units, collecting and analyzing the signals, classifying them in real time, and rendering the result back as touch. Recently that meant using IMU data to work out where on the body a device is worn so it can adapt its feedback, and building wearable actuators that can nudge how heavy a virtual object feels.

I like working in both directions at once. Research keeps me honest about whether an interaction actually works for a person, and development means I can build the apparatus, ship the thing, and measure it myself instead of stopping at a diagram. My strength is sticking with a problem — through the calibration curve that doesn't fit and the prototype that needs rebuilding — until it holds up.

🔬 Research — Haptics & Tactile Interfaces

My graduate work is on on-body haptic interfaces: building the hardware, collecting the signals, working out what they mean, and rendering the result as touch.

Sensing hardware & prototyping

I build sensing prototypes on Arduino Uno and Teensy 4.1 — designing the analog front end, wiring voltage-divider circuits, and reading them over ADC with serial logging.

I work with force-sensitive resistors (FSR), IMUs, and capacitive and optical proximity sensors, and I select components against manufacturer application notes rather than by spec-sheet headline: comparing capacitive skin-proximity parts against optical IR reflectance for wear detection, and accounting for the practical failure modes — temperature drift, sweat and water ingress, loose fit, and the shielding and substrate choices that decide whether a design survives them.

Data collection & analysis

I set up the measurement path end to end: instrument the sensor, log the raw stream, then calibrate it into physical units instead of trusting the vendor's example code.

For an FSR, that meant reverse-engineering resistance from the divider output across three different load resistors, confirming the recovered sensor resistance agreed between them, then fitting a log–log regression to recover the resistance–force power law and inverting it to read out grams-force — including characterizing where the fit breaks down at very low and saturating loads.

Algorithms & modeling

Real-time classification of sensor streams — most recently IMU-based body-part classification, so an on-body device can adapt its feedback to where it is worn. Presented as a first-author oral paper at the 4th Korea Haptics Conference (2026).

Also geometric and geospatial estimation: fitting circumradius over road geometry to recover curvature, and deriving cross-slope from it against the governing design standard.

Haptic rendering & implementation

Turning signals into felt sensation. I design haptic waveforms and event trains with Core Haptics, synthesize surface textures procedurally, and drive actuators from live input so intensity and sharpness track what the user is doing.

I also measure the system honestly — including round-trip haptic latency across paired devices, which is easy to hand-wave and hard to actually quantify.

Fabrication

3D printing and prototype fabrication for enclosures, sensor mounts, and wearable form factors — keeping the device small, quiet, and light enough to actually be worn, which usually constrains the design more than the electronics do.

XR

Multi-projection XR environments and spatial interaction, including LiDAR- and tracker-based sensor fusion for tracking a user and objects in a room-scale CAVE setup — the subject of a filed patent application.

💻 Development

Shipping the software side — apps in the App Store, on-device ML, and the tooling around them.

iOS (SwiftUI, UIKit)

My main platform. I build views and features to spec, and I've shipped and maintained live App Store services using Swift Concurrency and Alamofire.

Beyond UI, I work with the frameworks that make devices feel physical: Core Haptics for haptic design, Core Bluetooth for device-to-device links, Core Motion for gyroscope and device-motion input, and WatchKit for companion Apple Watch apps. I follow WWDC closely and pick up new APIs as they land.

On-device machine learning

Core ML and Vision for inference that runs entirely on the device, with no network round trip. I've converted PyTorch models to Core ML with Core ML Tools — including folding preprocessing into the model's image input — and compared candidate architectures before committing to one.

AR / VR (RealityKit, ARKit, visionOS)

Spatial apps and interaction: placing and animating objects in space, anchors, hand tracking, and Reality Composer Pro. I've built visionOS projects and tested AR features on iOS devices with the camera and LiDAR scanner.
