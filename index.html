I started as an app developer, shipping iOS and XR apps through the Apple Developer Academy and beyond. Building spatial apps that could be seen and heard but never felt pulled me into haptics research, where I now build the wearable sensors and haptic feedback systems myself, and ship the software behind them too.

More about how I got here

I learned to build by starting from someone's actual problem rather than from a feature list, and I shipped several apps to the App Store working alongside designers, planners, and engineers.

From there I moved toward XR. I built visionOS and ARKit projects, put objects into space, and made them respond to hands. But the more spatial apps I made, the more one gap stood out. Everything I built could be seen and heard, and almost nothing could be felt. A virtual object you can pick up but not feel is still a picture. That gap pulled me into HCI, because if I wanted interfaces that genuinely connect with people, I had to understand how people perceive and not only how devices render.

So I went to study it properly. My research is on on-body haptic interfaces: instrumenting sensors and calibrating them into real physical units, collecting and analyzing the signals, classifying them in real time, and rendering the result back as touch. Recently that meant using IMU data to work out where on the body a device is worn so it can adapt its feedback, and building wearable actuators that can nudge how heavy a virtual object feels.

I like working in both directions at once. Research keeps me honest about whether an interaction actually works for a person, and development means I can build the apparatus, ship the thing, and measure it myself instead of stopping at a diagram. My strength is sticking with a problem, through the calibration curve that doesn't fit and the prototype that needs rebuilding, until it holds up.

🔬 Research: Haptics & Tactile Interfaces

My graduate work is on on-body haptic interfaces: building the hardware, collecting the signals, working out what they mean, and rendering the result as touch.

🔌

Sensing hardware & prototyping

Sensing prototypes on Arduino Uno and Teensy 4.1: FSR, IMU, and capacitive/optical proximity sensors, with the analog front end and component selection driven by manufacturer application notes, not spec-sheet headlines.

⚙️

Haptic hardware fabrication & actuator control

I build the haptic hardware itself, not just the software that drives it. Hands-on with a range of actuators and materials: vibration motors (ERM, LRA, and similar), shape memory alloy (SMA) actuators, pneumatic actuation, and cast silicone including making my own molds, along with the driver circuits and control logic each one needs.

📊

Data collection & analysis

End-to-end measurement pipelines: instrument the sensor, log the raw stream, then calibrate it into physical units instead of trusting the vendor's example code. Collect from a range of sensors, accelerometers, IMUs, and pressure/FSR sensors among them, using both microcontrollers and dedicated DAQ hardware depending on the sampling rate and channel count the measurement needs. Recovered an FSR's resistance-to-force curve via log-log regression, cross-validated across three load resistors.

🧮

Algorithms & modeling

Real-time classification of sensor streams, most recently IMU-based body-part classification so an on-body device can adapt its feedback to where it's worn. First-author oral paper, 4th Korea Haptics Conference (2026).

🎛️

Haptic rendering & implementation

Haptic waveforms and event trains in Core Haptics, procedural surface textures, and actuators driven from live input so intensity and sharpness track the user in real time, including measured round-trip latency across paired devices.

🖨️

Fabrication

3D printing and prototype fabrication for enclosures, sensor mounts, and wearable form factors, small and light enough to actually be worn.

🥽

XR

Multi-projection XR environments and spatial interaction, including LiDAR- and tracker-based sensor fusion for a room-scale CAVE setup. Subject of a filed patent application.
