🔬 Research — Haptics & Tactile Interfaces

My graduate work is on on-body haptic interfaces: building the hardware, collecting the signals, working out what they mean, and rendering the result as touch.

Sensing hardware & prototyping

I build sensing prototypes on Arduino Uno and Teensy 4.1 — designing the analog front end, wiring voltage-divider circuits, and reading them over ADC with serial logging.

I work with force-sensitive resistors (FSR), IMUs, and capacitive and optical proximity sensors, and I select components against manufacturer application notes rather than by spec-sheet headline: comparing capacitive skin-proximity parts against optical IR reflectance for wear detection, and accounting for the practical failure modes — temperature drift, sweat and water ingress, loose fit, and the shielding and substrate choices that decide whether a design survives them.

Data collection & analysis

I set up the measurement path end to end: instrument the sensor, log the raw stream, then calibrate it into physical units instead of trusting the vendor's example code.

For an FSR, that meant reverse-engineering resistance from the divider output across three different load resistors, confirming the recovered sensor resistance agreed between them, then fitting a log–log regression to recover the resistance–force power law and inverting it to read out grams-force — including characterizing where the fit breaks down at very low and saturating loads.

Algorithms & modeling

Real-time classification of sensor streams — most recently IMU-based body-part classification, so an on-body device can adapt its feedback to where it is worn. Presented as a first-author oral paper at the 4th Korea Haptics Conference (2026).

Also geometric and geospatial estimation: fitting circumradius over road geometry to recover curvature, and deriving cross-slope from it against the governing design standard.

Haptic rendering & implementation

Turning signals into felt sensation. I design haptic waveforms and event trains with Core Haptics, synthesize surface textures procedurally, and drive actuators from live input so intensity and sharpness track what the user is doing.

I also measure the system honestly — including round-trip haptic latency across paired devices, which is easy to hand-wave and hard to actually quantify.

Fabrication

3D printing and prototype fabrication for enclosures, sensor mounts, and wearable form factors — keeping the device small, quiet, and light enough to actually be worn, which usually constrains the design more than the electronics do.

XR

Multi-projection XR environments and spatial interaction, including LiDAR- and tracker-based sensor fusion for tracking a user and objects in a room-scale CAVE setup — the subject of a filed patent application.
